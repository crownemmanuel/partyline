How it works

Up and talking before doors open

  1. 1

    Host on a laptop

    Open the PartyLine desktop app on any Mac, Windows or Linux machine. It starts the audio server, the control server and the admin console. Nothing to configure, nothing to sign up for.

  2. 2

    Crew joins with a code

    Phones on the same Wi‑Fi find the host automatically. Tap it, enter the 6‑digit join code (or scan the QR), type a name. Browsers just open the URL the host shows.

  3. 3

    Lock the phone. Talk.

    Plug in a headset and pocket the phone. PartyLine runs as an ongoing call, so it keeps working with the screen off. Tomorrow it reconnects on its own, no code needed.

For the producer

Talk to anyone. Hear only what you need.

The host app doubles as the admin console. Talk to one group, several groups, everyone, or a single person. Pick which groups you listen to and solo the ones that matter right now.

  • Talk to all, to a group, or to one device
  • Mute, rename, move or remove any device
  • Force hold‑to‑talk for the noisy ones
  • See who is speaking, battery, headset and latency

Groups

Camera talks to camera. Backstage stays backstage.

Split the team into groups. Each person talks and listens in their own group, and the server enforces who can hear whom, so backstage chatter never ends up in the camera operators' ears.

In the pocket

Built for a whole shift with the screen off.

On iPhone PartyLine uses CallKit, on Android a call‑style foreground service, so the system treats it like a phone call that never drops. Mute from the lock screen, or use the headset button as push‑to‑talk.

  • Keeps running with the phone locked
  • Headset button: latch or push‑to‑talk
  • Messages and attention buzz on the lock screen
  • Warns when there is no headset (echo risk)

Everything else

Small on purpose. Serious where it counts.

Low latency

Opus audio over WebRTC, forwarded by a local media server with no mixing step and no trip to the internet.

Local network only

Everything stays on your Wi‑Fi. Works in venues with no internet at all. Traffic is encrypted with a certificate the host makes on first run.

Join from anything

iPhone app, Android app, the desktop app on another computer, or any modern browser pointed at the host.

Text & quick replies

When nobody can talk: send text to a group or a person, answer with one tap — OK, Standby, Ready — or send an attention buzz.

Devices are remembered

Camera 2 today is Camera 2 tomorrow. Returning devices reconnect to their group without a join code.

Voice isolation

Noise suppression keeps the generator hum and crowd roar out of everyone else's ears.

FAQ

Questions crews ask

Is PartyLine really free?

Yes. PartyLine costs nothing: no accounts, no subscriptions, no per-user fees and no cloud service. Download the desktop app, put the app on your crew's phones and use it for as many shows as you like.

What is a partyline intercom?

A partyline is the shared talk channel a production crew uses to coordinate a show: everyone on the line hears everyone else, hands-free and in real time. PartyLine gives you that over Wi‑Fi, using phones and headsets instead of beltpacks and cables.

Can I use my phone as an intercom for my crew?

Yes. Install PartyLine on iPhone or Android, plug in a headset, and the phone becomes a full-duplex crew intercom with push-to-talk or open mic. Unlike walkie-talkie apps, people can talk and listen at the same time, and the audio stays on your local network.

Is PartyLine a free alternative to a wireless intercom system?

For small teams, yes. Instead of a base station and beltpacks, one laptop runs the PartyLine host and the crew use the phones they already own. You get groups, private talkback and push-to-talk at no cost. It covers whatever area your Wi‑Fi covers.

What do I need to get started?

One computer to host the show (macOS on Apple silicon, Windows or Linux), a Wi‑Fi network, and your crew's phones with headsets. Install the desktop app, share the 6-digit join code or QR code, and start talking.

Does it need the internet?

No. Everything runs between the host computer and the devices on the same Wi‑Fi network. A travel router with no internet connection works fine.

How many people can join?

PartyLine is designed for small production teams: about 3 to 30 devices, one show at a time.

Will it keep working when my phone is locked?

Yes, in the iPhone and Android apps. PartyLine runs as an ongoing call (CallKit on iPhone, a call-style foreground service on Android), so it keeps working for a whole shift. Browsers can't guarantee this, so anyone who pockets their phone should use the app.

Can the producer talk to just one person?

Yes. The producer can talk to everyone, one or more groups, or a single device, and choose which groups to listen to. Crew members can hold Reply to producer so only the producer hears them.

Who uses PartyLine?

Small teams that need to talk during a show: church and worship production teams, theatre and stage management, live events and concerts, broadcast and livestream crews, film sets, conference AV, school productions and sports venues.

Do I need special headsets?

No. Any wired or Bluetooth headset with a microphone works. A headset is strongly recommended, because on speaker the phone can echo, and PartyLine warns you about it.

Push-to-talk or open mic?

Both. Each person can hold to talk or latch the mic on. The producer can force hold-to-talk on any device.

Why does my browser warn about the certificate?

The host makes its own encryption certificate the first time it runs, because there is no internet authority to sign it on a private network. The native apps check the host by its fingerprint; in a browser you accept it once.
